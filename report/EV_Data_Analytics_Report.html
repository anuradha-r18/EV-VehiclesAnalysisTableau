Executive Summary

Key Findings at a Glance

Comprehensive analysis of 150,482 registered electric vehicles across the U.S. dataset

  • Tesla dominates with 68,983 vehicles — nearly 48.15% of the entire dataset, far outpacing second-place Nissan at 9.43%.
  • Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) account for 77.6% of the fleet, signaling a strong shift away from hybrid plug-ins.
  • Washington State is the overwhelming hub, home to 99.8% of all registered EVs in the dataset (150,141 vehicles).
  • Model Year 2023 recorded the highest registrations ever at 37,079 units — a 32.9% jump over 2022.
  • CAFV eligibility remains murky: 46.3% of vehicles have unknown eligibility, highlighting a data/policy gap.

    Strategic Insights & Observations
    🚀
    Tesla's Unmatched Dominance
    Tesla holds 48.15% of the market with 68,983 vehicles. The Model Y and Model 3 alone represent 37.35% of all EV registrations—a remarkable brand concentration in a competitive market.
    📈
    Explosive 2023 Growth
    Model year 2023 broke all records with 37,079 registrations, a 32.9% surge over 2022's 27,799. This growth suggests infrastructure maturation and increased consumer confidence in EV technology.
    🌿
    CAFV Policy Gap
    46.3% of vehicles have unknown CAFV eligibility status. This significant gap indicates that many owners may be missing out on clean fuel incentives due to lack of battery range data or research.
    🏙️
    King County Concentration
    King County (Seattle metro) accounts for 79,075 vehicles — 52.5% of all EVs. This urban concentration points to a need for suburban and rural charging infrastructure expansion.
    🔋
    BEV Adoption Accelerating
    Battery EVs dominate at 77.6% of the fleet, up from earlier years when PHEVs were more common. Consumer comfort with full electric range is clearly growing year over year.
    🌐
    Washington State Epicenter
    An overwhelming 99.8% of all registered EVs are in Washington State (150,141 of 150,482). The state's progressive EV policies and utility incentives are clearly bearing fruit at scale.
    09
    Conclusion

    This analysis of 150,482 electric vehicles reveals a rapidly maturing EV market anchored by Tesla's commanding presence and driven by Washington State's forward-thinking clean energy policy. The year-on-year growth trajectory — especially the 2023 peak of 37,079 registrations — underscores that EV adoption is not a trend but a structural shift in transportation.

    Key opportunities lie in resolving the CAFV eligibility data gap (46.3% unknown), expanding EV infrastructure beyond King County's urban core, and supporting emerging brands like Rivian and Mercedes-Benz that are beginning to capture market share.

    The future of mobility in Washington — and potentially beyond — is unequivocally electric.
